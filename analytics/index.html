Mercury contamination trends, 2009–2026

90.4% of all records were collected between 2017 and 2026, reflecting rapid growth in international surveillance programs. Despite enforcement actions and product recalls, violation rates have trended sharply upward — from 12% in 2017 to 88% in 2023.

🚨 The situation is worsening, not improving. The share of samples exceeding the 1 ppm legal threshold rose from 12% in 2017 to 88% in 2023. Pakistan has displaced Mexico and India as the dominant manufacturing source, now attributed to 52–70% of contaminated products. New toxic brands entered the market at 24–29 per year from 2022–2025 — enforcement has not deterred new entrants.

Geographic patterns of contamination

Records were collected across 31 countries and attributed to manufacturers in 45 countries. The United States leads in collection volume; Pakistan has become the dominant attributed manufacturing source from 2022 onward.

Product category breakdown

Cream products account for 79% (n=1,664) of all records. Soap, lotion and serum categories are tested far less frequently, limiting conclusions about their true contamination rates.

🧴 Surveillance bias note: The dataset reflects regulatory targeting priorities, not market share. Cream-form products present higher dermal absorption risk and have been the primary focus of testing. Categories with zero contamination (Gel, Butter) each have n≤23 samples — under-testing cannot be ruled out.

Key manufacturers identified in the dataset

16 manufacturers were traced from product labels. The dataset is dominated by a small cluster of Pakistani manufacturers concentrated in Swat/KPK and Lahore provinces, most of whom supply products found across multiple continents.

Intra-brand variance — counterfeit & adulteration signals

When the same brand tests at wildly different concentrations in different markets, three explanations apply: (1) different SKUs or formulations for different markets; (2) post-manufacture adulteration in the supply chain; or (3) deliberate region-specific toxic formulation. The article confirmed that Pond's US was post-manufacture adulteration.

🔬 Extreme variance ratios indicate counterfeit or adulterated supply chains. Faiza shows a >36 million× range (0.001 ppm in Belgium vs 36,000 ppm in US samples). Goree shows a 303,913× range. These are not measurement errors — they demonstrate that the same brand name is applied to fundamentally different products in different markets.

🔐 Policy implication: Brand-level monitoring is insufficient without supply-chain tracking. A "clean" test of one Faiza or Goree unit does not confirm that another unit purchased in the same week at the same store will be safe. Lot-based testing and customs seizure linked to specific production batches would be more effective than point-of-sale sampling.
